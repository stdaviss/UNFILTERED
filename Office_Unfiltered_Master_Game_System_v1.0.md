# OFFICE UNFILTERED™

## Master Game System & Production Specification

### Version 1.0 --- Product Development Master Document

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 0. DOCUMENT PURPOSE

This document is the master specification for building **Office
Unfiltered™** from concept through physical production, playtesting,
digital adaptation, and eventual commercial launch.

It is intended to serve as the single source of truth for:

-   Game design
-   Card content
-   Rules and mechanics
-   Safety and consent
-   Facilitation
-   Visual identity
-   Character system
-   Physical card production
-   Prototype development
-   Playtesting
-   Future digital implementation
-   Future industry and age adaptations

**Core product idea:**

> Office Unfiltered™ is a play-based human connection game that uses
> humor, recognition, roleplay, creativity, and structured interaction
> to help people bond in workplace environments without forcing
> vulnerability.

The game is designed to feel like a more mature, workplace-aware
evolution of familiar social games such as Truth or Dare, while
remaining suitable for professional environments.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 1. PRODUCT VISION

## 1.1 What We Are Building

Office Unfiltered™ is not simply a deck of questions.

It is a **structured social game system** designed around:

-   Human connection
-   Shared recognition
-   Humor
-   Play
-   Safe self-expression
-   Workplace culture
-   Team interaction
-   Consent
-   Psychological and physical safety

The experience should make players think:

> "That is so true."

> "We have all experienced that."

> "I can't believe this game actually asked that."

> "That was actually fun."

without making anyone feel:

> "I am being exposed."

> "I have to reveal something personal."

> "I am being judged."

# 3. CORE PHILOSOPHY

Office Unfiltered™ is built on five non-negotiable principles.

## 3.1 Shared Recognition \> Forced Vulnerability

The game should create connection by making people recognize shared
experiences rather than requiring personal confessions.

## 3.2 Safe Distance Enables Honesty

Hypotheticals, scenarios, archetypes, fictional situations, and humor
allow people to express ideas without having to expose themselves.

## 3.3 Opt-Out Is Power

Every player retains the ability to stop, skip, reframe, or decline
participation without explanation.

## 3.4 Play Before Reflection

The game should create laughter and energy first. Deeper reflection is
optional.

## 3.5 Facilitation Over Chaos

The rules should create enough structure that players can relax and have
fun.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 4. WHAT OFFICE UNFILTERED™ IS NOT

Office Unfiltered™ is:

-   Not therapy
-   Not conflict resolution
-   Not an employee evaluation tool
-   Not a performance assessment
-   Not a psychological diagnostic tool
-   Not a forced-vulnerability exercise
-   Not a substitute for professional mediation
-   Not a mechanism for managers to extract personal information

No card should be used to assess employee performance, loyalty, mental
health, relationships, or suitability for employment.

# 7. CARD TAXONOMY

## 7.1 TRUTH

Purpose:

-   Recognition
-   Observation
-   Reflection
-   Workplace humor
-   Shared experience

Truth questions should primarily examine:

-   Systems
-   Habits
-   Communication
-   Workplace culture
-   Productivity
-   Energy
-   Motivation
-   Meaning

They should not require intimate disclosure.

## 7.2 DARE / TASK

Purpose:

-   Energy
-   Expression
-   Creativity
-   Humor
-   Movement
-   Communication

Dares must remain:

-   Optional
-   Physically safe
-   Emotionally safe
-   Non-sexual
-   Non-humiliating
-   Achievable without special skills

## 7.3 SCENARIO

Purpose:

-   Simulate recognizable workplace situations
-   Encourage group interpretation
-   Create humor
-   Encourage discussion

Scenarios should be fictional and avoid real individuals.

## 7.4 ARCHETYPE

Purpose:

-   Roleplay
-   Psychological distance
-   Perspective-taking
-   Humor
-   Alternative viewpoints

Players respond as a character or behavioral archetype rather than as
themselves.

## 7.5 SAFETY

Purpose:

-   Give players control
-   Reset energy
-   Permit skipping
-   Reframe participation
-   Pause or end the game

Safety cards override normal game flow.

# 9. FACILITATOR

A facilitator may be:

-   HR representative
-   Team leader
-   Event host
-   External facilitator
-   Founder
-   Rotating player

For casual groups, a dedicated facilitator is optional.

For corporate events, a facilitator is recommended.

## Facilitator Responsibilities

The facilitator:

-   Explains the rules
-   Establishes consent
-   Maintains pace
-   Controls energy
-   Enforces safety
-   Stops inappropriate behavior
-   Handles uncomfortable moments
-   Can veto a card
-   Can end the game

The facilitator must never use the game to pressure a participant.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 10. OPENING RITUAL

Before play begins, the facilitator should communicate:

> This is a game, not therapy.

> You can skip any card without explaining why.

> Keep real names and identifiable incidents out of the game.

> We are here to have fun, not evaluate each other.

> If something doesn't feel right, skip it.

The facilitator should make skipping feel normal rather than
exceptional.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 11. CONSENT SYSTEM

Consent is continuous.

A player can:

-   Participate
-   Skip
-   Reframe
-   Answer hypothetically
-   Ask for another card
-   Pause
-   Leave the game

No player must justify any of these choices.

## No Forced Participation

Never require:

-   Personal disclosure
-   Physical contact
-   Eye contact
-   Singing
-   Dancing
-   Public speaking
-   Imitation
-   Sharing trauma
-   Sharing confidential work information

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 12. UNIVERSAL SAFETY RULES

The following are prohibited:

-   Naming coworkers negatively
-   Revealing confidential company information
-   Revealing confidential client information
-   Sexualized tasks
-   Forced physical contact
-   Dangerous physical tasks
-   Tasks involving alcohol or drugs
-   Tasks involving weapons
-   Tasks involving climbing, jumping, running, or risky movement
-   Humiliation
-   Mocking physical appearance
-   Mocking disability
-   Mocking race, ethnicity, nationality, religion, gender, sexuality,
    age, or other protected characteristics
-   Personal attacks
-   Performance scoring
-   Recording participants without consent

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 13. PHYSICAL SAFETY

Default physical rule:

> If a task could reasonably cause injury, it does not belong in the
> standard deck.

Dares should generally be:

-   Seated
-   Standing in place
-   Small-range movement
-   Gesture-based
-   Voice-based
-   Imagination-based

No contact is required.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 14. PSYCHOLOGICAL SAFETY

Avoid cards that ask players to:

-   Reveal trauma
-   Reveal medical information
-   Reveal relationship problems
-   Reveal financial hardship
-   Confess wrongdoing
-   Identify disliked coworkers
-   Identify incompetent coworkers
-   Reveal confidential workplace issues
-   Discuss personal mental-health conditions
-   Reveal sexuality
-   Reveal political or religious beliefs

The game should produce **recognition rather than exposure**.

# 16. TURN FLOW

Standard turn:

1.  Player draws a card.
2.  Player reads it aloud.
3.  Player performs or answers.
4.  Group reacts briefly.
5.  Facilitator moves the game forward.
6.  Next player takes a turn.

The game should avoid unnecessary analysis.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 17. ARCHETYPE MECHANIC

When an Archetype card is drawn, the player may:

-   Adopt the archetype for the next card
-   Assign the archetype to another willing player
-   Use it for a group response
-   Return it to the deck depending on the selected mode

Example:

**THE BIG-PICTURE THINKER**

The next response should be given from the perspective of someone
focused on long-term outcomes rather than immediate details.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 18. SAFETY CARD MECHANIC

Safety cards may be drawn naturally or deliberately introduced by the
facilitator.

A Safety card overrides normal play instructions.

Examples:

-   Skip
-   Replace
-   Reframe
-   Group response
-   Pause
-   Lower energy
-   End game

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 19. GAME ENDING

A session may end:

-   At a predetermined time
-   After a set number of rounds
-   When the facilitator calls it
-   When the group collectively agrees
-   When an End/Closing safety card appears

## Closing Ritual

Optional final prompt:

> "One word for how that felt."

No one is required to answer.

The game should end on a positive note rather than a forced debrief.

# 24. COMPLETE SAFETY DECK

Safety cards override ordinary game flow.

### Safety 01 --- Skip, No Explanation Needed

You may skip this card or any future card without giving a reason.

### Safety 02 --- Answer Hypothetically

You may answer as "someone," "a team," or "in general," not as yourself.

### Safety 03 --- Replace the Card

Discard this card and draw a new one immediately.

### Safety 04 --- Group Response Only

Everyone answers together. No individual spotlight.

### Safety 05 --- Pause the Game

The facilitator pauses for a short break or reset.

### Safety 06 --- Change the Energy Level

Switch to Light Mode (Truth + Scenario only) for the next 5 turns.

### Safety 07 --- Humor Mode

For the next round, answers must stay playful and non-serious.

### Safety 08 --- Silent Round

For the next card, responses are shown with gestures or one word only.

### Safety 09 --- Facilitator's Choice

The facilitator decides how the next card is handled: skip, group
answer, or replace.

### Safety 10 --- End with Appreciation

The game can be closed immediately with one optional round of
appreciation or gratitude.

# 26. PHYSICAL CARD SIZE

## Standard Finished Card

**63 mm × 88 mm**

Equivalent approximately:

**2.5 × 3.5 inches**

This is a standard poker-style playing-card proportion and is suitable
for:

-   Shuffling
-   Handling
-   Standard card boxes
-   Professional card manufacturers
-   Casual group play

## Bleed

Use:

**3 mm bleed on all sides**

Therefore the design canvas should be:

**69 mm × 94 mm**

The final cut remains:

**63 mm × 88 mm**

Important:

-   Background artwork extends through the bleed.
-   Text and critical graphics remain inside the safe area.
-   Character faces and important details should never sit close to the
    trim line.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 27. PRINT SAFE ZONE

Maintain approximately:

**3--5 mm internal safety margin from the final trim.**

Do not place:

-   Text
-   Logos
-   Faces
-   Important icons
-   Category labels

too close to the trim.

# 39. PLAYTESTING PROTOCOL

The first physical prototype should not aim for perfect production
quality.

Use simple printed cards first.

Recommended first test groups:

1.  Friends who work together or know office culture
2.  Mixed group of people who do not work together
3.  Small professional/corporate group

Recommended group size:

**4--8 people**

Record observations.

Do not immediately defend a card when players react negatively.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 40. WHAT TO MEASURE DURING PLAYTESTS

Track:

### Flow

-   Did players understand what to do?
-   Were turns too long?
-   Did anyone get confused?

### Energy

-   Where did laughter happen?
-   Where did energy drop?
-   Which cards created excitement?

### Safety

-   Did anyone hesitate?
-   Did any card create discomfort?
-   Did players naturally skip?
-   Did people feel pressured?

### Social Dynamics

-   Did one person dominate?
-   Did quieter people participate?
-   Did managers change how employees behaved?
-   Did people become more relaxed?

### Replayability

Ask:

> "Would you play this again?"

And:

> "Which card did you remember most?"

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 41. PLAYTEST FEEDBACK FORM

Recommended questions:

1.  How fun was the game from 1--10?
2.  How comfortable did you feel playing?
3.  Which card type did you enjoy most?
4.  Which card type did you enjoy least?
5.  Was anything confusing?
6.  Did any card feel uncomfortable?
7.  Did the game feel too corporate, too casual, or balanced?
8.  Did you feel pressured to participate?
9.  Would you play again?
10. Would you recommend it to a team?
11. What should we remove?
12. What should we add?
13. Which moment was funniest?
14. Which moment felt most natural?
15. Which moment felt forced?
